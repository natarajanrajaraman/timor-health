# What this document cannot do

**It has no official standing.** Nothing here is endorsed by the Ministry of Health, WHO, or any
government or institution. It is a private compilation of public sources.

**It is a desk review.** No fieldwork, no interviews, no primary data collection. It reports what
published sources say and where they disagree. It cannot tell you what a particular municipality needs
this year, and it is no substitute for talking to Timorese counterparts.

**It contains no assessment of where partners are most needed.** That section is deliberately omitted
from this edition — it would be judgement rather than sourced description — and its absence is
disclosed on the page rather than passed over silently.

# Known gaps — things that simply are not published

These are not oversights in this document. They are gaps in the public record for Timor-Leste.

1. **No 3W ("who does what where") dataset and no health-cluster partner list.** Confirmed with a
   positive control. The actor map in §8 exists only because it was assembled by hand.
2. **No current national HRH plan.** The 2020–2024 plan has expired with no published successor.
3. **No published bed counts** for any hospital. The HNGV figure in §7 comes from a single academic
   paper, and is the only one found.
4. **Graduate output by profession, and retention in the Timorese health workforce, is not published.**
   Only gross output across all six training institutions is available — see §6 on why that is a poor
   proxy.
5. **Allied health training is essentially undocumented** in authoritative sources.
6. **Whether the December 2023 national TB prevalence survey results have been published is
   UNVERIFIED.** For a country with among the world's highest TB incidence, this would be the most
   consequential epidemiological release available.
7. **The legal instrument behind the INSP-TL training and research approval requirement is untraced.**
   The requirement is real and well attested by practitioners; its statutory basis is not established
   here.
8. **The five private health-training institutions counted by WHO are not named by WHO**, and this
   document could not close that gap authoritatively.
9. **Several significant organisations have no verifiable published contact** — including KP+, arguably
   the most operationally important HIV civil-society organisation in the country.

# Known contradictions, left unresolved on purpose

Where authoritative sources disagree, this document reports the disagreement rather than choosing.

- **Skilled birth attendance moves in opposite directions** depending on whether you use the routine
  information system or the household survey (§3). Unresolved, and arguably the most useful thing here.
- **Two WHO products disagree on health expenditure by roughly 60%**, and a third widely-circulated WHO
  figure cannot be reproduced from any published primitive (§5).
- **WHO's own UHC service coverage index differs between products** — 48 in the Observatory, 52 in the
  Country Cooperation Strategy (§3).
- **Level-III community health centre counts differ between two WHO sources** — 9 or 10 (§7).
- **Facility counts vary substantially across sources and years** (§7). Some of this is real network
  growth; some is inconsistent definition.

# What ages fastest

**In order of decay:**

1. **Contact details and organisational status in §8.** A meaningful share of Timorese health
   organisations have no working website, several domains have lapsed, and at least two actively mislead
   an automated checker. **Assume anything in §8 may be out of date and verify before relying on it.**
2. **Named office-holders.** Leadership names appear in WHO and national documents, and at least one is
   already inconsistent across sources. Confirm current post-holders directly.
3. **Programme and grant status.** Both current Global Fund grants end **31 December 2026**.
4. **Budget figures**, which change annually.
5. **The structural description of the system**, which is the most stable material here — though note
   the planned municipal hospital tier in §4 that does not yet exist.

# On the translation

The executive summary is provided in **Tetun by machine translation** and is labelled as such. The named
editor reviews the English text and **does not certify the Tetun**. Where precision matters, use the
English and consult a human translator.

Read the banner at the top of the page. It states whether this edition has been reviewed by the named

continuing to call itself a landscape scan.**
