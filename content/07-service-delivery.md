# The three tiers, as they exist today

*Verbatim from WHO CCS 2026–2030 §2.3:*

> "Health services in Timor-Leste are organized at three levels of care –
> • Primary health care facilities constituting a network of **344 health posts (HPs)** and **61
>   community health-care centres (level I & II)** at sub-municipal levels;
> • Secondary health-care facilities constituting **level-III CHCs (10 in number)**, **one regional
>   hospital (Baucau)** and **four referral hospitals (at Maubisse, Suai, Maliana and Oecusse)** at
>   municipal levels;
> • Tertiary level of care, which at present is limited to **one national hospital HNGV**, located in
>   Dili."

**In summary: 6 hospitals · about 71 community health centres · 344 health posts.**

⚠️ **One discrepancy worth knowing.** The CCS says 10 level-III CHCs; WHO's own 2023 NCD facility survey
sampling frame says 9. Both are WHO, about 2.5 years apart. **Treat "9–10" as the honest range** — and
treat this as a small illustration of a general rule for Timorese facility data.

**Facility counts differ substantially between sources and over time**, which is normal for a growing
network but catches people out:

| Count | Source | Date |
|---|---|---|
| 306 HPs fully functioning; 71% with water supply | NHSSP II 2020–2030 | ~2020 |
| 71 CHCs, 318 HPs, 7 treatment posts, 600 SISCa outposts | commonly cited | 2021 |
| 71 CHCs, ~440 village HPs, 5 referral hospitals, 1 national | medRxiv protocol supplement | 2023 |
| **344 HPs, 71 CHCs, 5 hospitals + HNGV** | **WHO CCS 2026–2030** | **2026** |

The network has slightly overshot its own decade-old PENSS target of 313 health posts and 70 CHCs.

# Hospitals

## Hospital Nacional Guido Valadares (HNGV), Dili

The country's **sole tertiary facility**, and an **autonomous institution** rather than a line
directorate.

- **Beds: 260–340.** ⚠️ This comes from a single published study (Salendo et al., *Medicine* 101(50),
  December 2022, DOI 10.1097/md.0000000000032113). **It is the only published bed figure found — the
  Ministry publishes none.**
- Handles most of the country's **haemodialysis**.
- Timor-Leste's **first indigenous medical oxygen plant** was installed here in 2025 and began
  functioning in **February 2026**.
- A new **paediatric and intensive coronary care building**, valued at over **US$2.4 million**, is under
  construction (General State Budget 2026).
- Complex interventions are still **referred overseas** — see §5 on the US$19.3 million overseas
  treatment line.

## Regional and referral hospitals

| Hospital | Municipality | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| **Eduardo Ximenes Regional Hospital** | Baucau | **Regional** |
| **Maliana Referral Hospital** | Bobonaro | Referral |
| **Maubisse Referral Hospital** | Ainaro | Referral |
| **Suai Referral Hospital** | Cova Lima | Referral |
| **Oecusse Referral Hospital** (Pante Macassar) | RAEOA | Referral |

⚠️ **Baucau is a regional hospital, not a referral hospital.** The flat five-item list that most
documents repeat flattens a real distinction in the referral chain.

All five have out-patient, emergency and in-patient departments, staffed with general practitioners and
specialists in **four clinical areas: surgery, paediatrics, obstetrics and gynaecology, and internal
medicine**. Each is separately managed with its own executive director.

**Critical care units have been established across all five**, with WHO having trained **over 1,000
doctors and nurses** in basic emergency and critical care. A **newborn birth-defect online database**
now runs in all five.

# Primary care and community delivery

**Community health centres (CHCs, *Sentru Saúde Komunitáriu* — SSK)** operate at three levels; levels I
and II are sub-municipal, level III is a secondary facility.

**Health posts (*Postu Saúde* — PS)** are the front line, 344 of them.

**SISCa** — *Serbisu Integradu Saúde Komunitária* — is the integrated community outreach service, the
mechanism by which services reach populations that cannot reach facilities. Around **600 SISCa
outposts** have been cited.

**PSF — community health volunteers** — are registered nationally, with **600+** reported. They are a
significant part of how primary care actually reaches households, and are worth understanding before
designing any community-level intervention.

**Saúde na Família** is the national family-health approach to primary care. Its adoption was directly
inspired by the Cuban training model (§6) — the connection between how Timorese doctors were trained and
how primary care is organised is not incidental.

# Priority programmes

The four stated national priority areas are **reproductive, maternal, newborn and child health**;
**malnutrition**; **communicable disease, particularly TB and HIV**; and **non-communicable disease**.
Epidemiology for each is in §3.

Programme-level notes:

- **TB** carries the largest communicable-disease burden (496/100,000, 2024) and is covered by a Global
  Fund grant with the Ministry as Principal Recipient, **running to 31 December 2026**.
- **Malaria** has moved from elimination to **prevention of re-establishment** following certification
  in July 2025 — the Global Fund grant is named accordingly, also **ending 31 December 2026**.
- **Immunisation** is comparatively well instrumented: DHIS2-tracked, HPV rolled out from July 2024, and
  a digital e-tracker launched June 2026.
- **NCD services readiness** was assessed nationally in the 2023 facility survey (§3) — the closest
  thing the country has to a facility-readiness baseline.

⚠️ **Both current Global Fund grants end on 31 December 2026.** If your planning assumes their
continuation, check the position rather than assuming it.

# Health information system

**TLHIS, the Timor-Leste Health Information System, is DHIS2-based.** WHO records that it *"was upgraded
to the latest version and revamped to include indicators from key programmatic areas such as
immunization, reproductive-maternal-newborn-child and adolescent health, school health, nutrition, NCDs
and communicable disease"*.

Significant work is underway to **integrate health workforce data (SIRUS) into the HMIS**.

⚠️ **The routine system is also one side of the skilled-birth-attendance contradiction in §3.** A
functioning DHIS2 instance does not by itself resolve data-quality questions, and the honest reading of
§3 is that at least one of the country's two headline maternal-health data streams is not measuring what
its label says.
